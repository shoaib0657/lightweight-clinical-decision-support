# Clinician-facing radiology summary

This educational fixture describes how to summarize a radiology report for a clinical reader. Preserve the examination type and anatomical area, the reason stated for the examination, the named comparison study, key positive and negative findings, the radiologist's impression, and any explicitly stated follow-up. Separate direct report content from contextual notes supplied by another clinician. Do not infer a diagnosis, certainty level, or recommendation that is absent from the source report. If the report contains an ambiguity or an unresolved recommendation, carry that limitation into the summary.

# Patient-facing radiology explanation

A patient-facing explanation uses plain language while preserving the meaning and uncertainty of the original report. Define necessary technical terms, distinguish a radiology finding from a final clinical diagnosis, and say when the report recommends discussion or follow-up with the treating professional. Do not add reassurance, alarm, treatment advice, or a diagnosis that the radiologist did not provide. Questions that require the broader medical record should be identified as evidence gaps rather than answered from the imaging report alone.
